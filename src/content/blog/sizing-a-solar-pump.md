Solar pumps are often sized backwards: the farmer buys "a 3 hp system" and only then works out whether it delivers
enough water. Sizing should start from the crop.

## 1. Peak daily water demand

For 2 ha of tomatoes at peak ET of 6 mm/day and 90% drip efficiency, the demand is
`2 × 10,000 × 0.006 / 0.9 ≈ 133 m³/day`.

## 2. Total dynamic head

Add the static lift, the drawdown, friction losses and the pressure the emitters need. Here that came to 48 m.

## 3. Hydraulic power and array size

Hydraulic energy is about `133 × 48 / 367 ≈ 17.4 kWh/day`. At 4.5 peak sun hours and around 45% wire-to-water
efficiency, that needs roughly **8.6 kWp** of panels. The farmer had been quoted 4 kWp.

## Don't skip

- Check the borewell yield with a pump test before choosing a flow rate
- Size the cables for less than 3% voltage drop
