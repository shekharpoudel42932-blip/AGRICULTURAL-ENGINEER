## Context

The study comprehensively assessed the techno-economic and environmental (TEE) feasibility of developing a solar PV system at Purbanchal University, Nepal. Four distinct scenarios, namely S1 (rooftop with fixed orientation), S2 (rooftop with bi-annual tilt orientation), S3 (rooftop and ground with fixed orientation), and S4 (rooftop and ground with bi-annual tilt orientation) were evaluated using PVsyst version 7.4.8 software.
## Study

This research compared fixed-tilt and seasonally adjusted tilt rooftop PV systems on technical, economic and environmental grounds. According to the GIS information, the latitude of the location of Purbanchal University is 26.68° North. Therefore, a fixed inclination angle of the PV module (27°) facing south was selected for the fixed-mounted PV structure scenario and for the single-axis mounted scenario, a tilt angle (latitude ± 15°) of 12° in summer and 42° in winter was chosen. The azimuth angle for the PV module was selected to be 0° to perfectly align the module with geographical south. 

## Findings
 Scenarios S1 and S2 are focused on meeting the energy demands, i.e., 239 MWh, and optimizing the utilization of the selected area with fixed and seasonal bi-annual tilt mounting systems, whereas S3 and S4 scenarios are focused on producing the maximum energy potential of the chosen location. The investment for S3 and S4 was found to be almost ten times higher than for S1 and S2; however, the return on investment for S1 and S2 is greater than that of S3 and S4. Results suggested that the optimal 202 kWp solar PV plant requires only 1,187 m² within the rooftop area to fulfill the university's annual electricity demand. Utilizing the rooftop and ground area, about 3,405 MWh can be produced by the system in S3. The highest replaced CO2 emissions were estimated in S2 and S4, respectively.
