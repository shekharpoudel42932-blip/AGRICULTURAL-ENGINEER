## Objective

This study evaluates the impact of government-subsidized shallow tubewell irrigation schemes implemented through the Agriculture Knowledge Centre (AKC), Banke, on agricultural transformation and farmer livelihoods across six municipalities of Banke District, Lumbini Province, Nepal. A cross-sectional survey of 120 beneficiary farmers was conducted using structured questionnaires administered via KoboCollect, capturing data on socio-demographic profiles, technical characteristics of installed systems, economic outcomes, and social dimensions of irrigation governance.

## Method

In this study, a comprehensive survey form, is designed to evaluate the effectiveness of government-supported irrigation subsidies among local farmers using KoboToolbox software (https://www.kobotoolbox.org/ ) and administered through KoboCollect mobile application during field visits with help of enumerators. The survey captured information on beneficiary demographics, irrigation type, subsidy utilization, farm size, and productivity changes, and the data were exported for statistical analysis to assess the effectiveness and impact of the scheme. The document is structured into specific modules that capture a respondent profile alongside detailed technical, economic, and social assessments regarding the use of shallow tubewells and pumping equipment.
The survey was conducted across several municipalities, including Kohalpur municipality, Rapti Sonari rural municipality, Khajura rural municipality, Duduwa rural municipality, Baijanath rural municipality, and Nepalgunj sub-metropolitan city. 

## Findings
The findings  reveal structural constraints that limit the full realisation of the subsidy scheme’s transformative potential. The extremely low rate of formal O&M training (21.7%) is a critical gap, as untrained operators are more prone to equipment failures that cause irrigation disruptions at agronomically critical periods. The weakness of Water User Associations, the prevalence of unequal water access, and the limited market connectivity available to roughly one-third of beneficiaries collectively constrain farmers’ ability to convert improved water security into equivalent income gains. The very low adoption of high-value crops (5.0%) and climate-smart agricultural practices (15.0%) further indicates that irrigation alone, while necessary, is not sufficient to drive comprehensive agricultural modernisation without parallel investments in extension, credit, and market infrastructure.


## Personal observation
While macroeconomic and historical literature often characterizes the eastern Gangetic plains and Nepal Terai as groundwater-abundant regions constrained only by economic access, my direct observations of widespread shallow tube well (STW) depletion reveal a more complex, evolving hydrogeological crisis. Across Terai districts, shallow aquifers are failing seasonally. This localized depletion is not merely a consequence of changing climate patterns, but a multi-tiered management failure: the degradation of critical upstream recharge capacity in the Chure-Bhabar range, coupled with an unregulated shift toward solar-powered over-extraction and competitive deep-boring. Witnessing this discrepancy between broad regional assumptions and local reality has driven my desire to join Groundwatch. I want to leverage data-driven monitoring and integrated watershed management to transition the Terai from localized, reactive exploitation toward long-term, managed aquifer recharge.
